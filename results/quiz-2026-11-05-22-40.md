1. **In which way may an altimeter subscale which is set to an incorrect QNH lead to an incorrect altimeter reading?** _(80-056, Altimeter, altitude references and VSI)_  
   - Your answer: If the subscale is set to a lower than actual pressure, the indication is too low. This may lead to much closer proximity to the ground than intended  
   - Why wrong: A lower setting causes an under-reading, so actual altitude is higher than indicated rather than dangerously lower.  
   - Correct: If the subscale is set to a higher than actual pressure, the indication is too high. This may lead to much closer proximity to the ground than intended — A higher-than-actual subscale setting makes the altimeter over-read, risking proximity to terrain.
2. **Given the following information, what is the true altitude? (rounded to the nearest 50 ft) QNH: 983 hPa Altitude: FL 85 Outside Air Temperature: ISA - 10°** _(30-019, Pressure and Altimetry)_  
   - Your answer: 7600 ft  
   - Why wrong: 7600 ft is the tempting pressure-only approximation; the bank expects the cold correction too.  
   - Correct: 7300 ft — FL85 = 8500 ft; QNH 983 is about 30 hPa below standard (≈900 ft), giving ≈7600 ft, then the bank’s ISA−10°C cold correction gives the keyed 7300 ft.
   - Remember: 7300 ft is the bank key; the result uses approximate pressure and cold-temperature corrections, so follow any supplied exam table.
3. **What information can be found in the ATIS, but not in a METAR?** _(30-140, Aviation Weather Reports)_  
   - Your answer: Approach information, such as ground visibility and cloud base  
   - Why wrong: METAR includes visibility and cloud base, so these are not unique ATIS details.  
   - Correct: Operational information such as runway in use and transition level — ATIS adds operational details such as runway in use and transition level.
4. **Which statement about induced drag during the horizontal cruise flight is correct?** _(51-016, Induced and parasite drag)_  
   - Your answer: Induced drag increases with increasing airspeed  
   - Why wrong: It decreases, rather than increases, with increasing speed in level cruise.  
   - Correct: Induced drag decreases with increasing airspeed — At a fixed level-flight lift requirement, induced drag decreases as airspeed increases.
