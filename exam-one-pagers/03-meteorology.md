# Meteorology — course-topic summary

[← Back to index](README.md)

**Course scope:** atmosphere and heat; temperature and pressure; circulation and wind; thermodynamics and stability; clouds and air masses; fronts; fog, precipitation, icing, thunderstorms and mountain weather; reports, charts and VFR planning.

## 1. Atmosphere, temperature, and pressure

Most weather occurs in the **troposphere**, where temperature generally decreases with height; the tropopause height varies with latitude and season. ISA is a reference: MSL 15°C, 1013.25 hPa, density 1.225 kg/m³, with a tropospheric lapse rate near 2°C/1000 ft (6.5°C/km). Distinguish the **environmental lapse rate** (actual surrounding air) from the **adiabatic rate** of a moving parcel.

The Sun heats Earth unevenly. Radiation heats the surface; conduction warms air touching it; convection transfers heat vertically; advection transports warm/cold air horizontally. Evaporation absorbs latent heat, while condensation releases it and slows the cooling of a saturated rising parcel. Surface type, cloud, wind, latitude, time and season affect temperature. Inversions—ground/radiation, subsidence or frontal—can trap haze/moisture and create low-level wind shear.

Pressure is the weight of the air column. Isobars show equal pressure: tight spacing means a stronger pressure gradient and generally stronger wind. A trough/ridge extends low/high pressure. Pressure falls with altitude non-linearly; the classroom 27–30 ft per hPa conversion is only a local approximation. **QNH** is reduced to MSL using ISA; **QFF** is reduced using actual conditions; **QFE** references a local datum; **QNE** is standard pressure. Cold air makes true altitude lower than indicated; low pressure also reduces true altitude for a given setting.

## 2. Wind, circulation, stability, and cloud

Pressure-gradient force starts wind from high toward low pressure; Coriolis deflects it right in the Northern Hemisphere and left in the Southern. Aloft, wind tends to follow isobars; at the surface, friction slows and turns it toward low pressure. Tight isobars imply stronger flow. Near-surface terrain, sea/land contrasts and heating modify wind: anabatic daytime upslope, katabatic night drainage, sea/land breezes, channelled winds, and foehn warming/drying downwind of mountains. Turbulence may be convective, mechanical/shear, orographic, clear-air, or wake-related.

Stability compares the parcel's temperature/lapse rate with the environment. A dry rising parcel cools about **3°C/1000 ft**; a saturated parcel cools more slowly, about **1.5°C/1000 ft** (both approximate and condition-dependent). Compare the environmental lapse rate with the dry/saturated adiabatic rates: stable air resists vertical displacement and favours layered cloud, haze and smoother flow; unstable air continues rising and favours cumulus, showers, thermals and turbulence. Moisture is measured through vapour pressure, mixing ratio and relative humidity (`actual vapour / saturation amount × 100%`); **dew point** is the temperature at which air becomes saturated by cooling. A small temperature/dew-point spread signals near saturation, not an exact cloud-base forecast.

Clouds form when air cools to saturation or gains moisture. Stable air tends to stratiform cloud; unstable air to cumuliform cloud. Recognize Ci/Cs/Cc (high), As/Ac (middle), St/Sc/Ns (low/layered), Cu and Cb (vertical/convection).

| Typical feature | Warm front | Cold front |
|---|---|---|
| Lift | Gentle ascent over cold air | Cold air undercuts warm air more steeply |
| Cloud | High cloud thickens/lowers into layers | Cumuliform cloud, potentially CB |
| Weather | Widespread, prolonged precipitation and poor visibility | Narrower band of showers, gusts and turbulence |

These are typical patterns, not a substitute for observing the actual front.

Air-mass characteristics begin at the source region (continental/maritime, polar/tropical) and change over the route. Warm moist air over a cold surface is cooled and stabilized; cold air over a warmer surface is heated from below and destabilized. A polar-front depression develops through wave, mature-front and occlusion stages; pressure tendency and wind shifts help locate passage. In a typical Northern Hemisphere surface low, winds circulate counter-clockwise and inward; around a high, clockwise and outward, modified by friction and local terrain.

## 3. Hazards and weather interpretation

| Fog | Formation |
|---|---|
| Radiation | Clear night, moist ground and light wind favour cooling to saturation |
| Advection | Moist air moves over a colder surface |
| Orographic | Moist air rises over terrain and cools |
| Frontal | Rain evaporates into cooler air below a front |
| Steaming | Cold air moves over warmer water |

Precipitation may be continuous/stratiform or showery/convective. METAR visibility is not the same as runway visual range or an airborne slant view.

Airframe icing requires supercooled water and cold aircraft surfaces: clear/glaze ice forms from larger drops; rime from smaller drops; mixed ice combines them. Ice distorts aerofoil shape, increases drag and can reduce control/stall margins. Carburettor icing is a separate induction hazard caused by venturi and fuel-vaporization cooling, possible in moist air over a broad temperature range. Remove contamination before flight; use only approved aircraft procedures.

Thunderstorms/CB bring severe turbulence, icing, hail, lightning, heavy precipitation and wind shear. Mature cells contain both strong up- and downdraughts. Mountain waves, rotors and lee turbulence may occur downwind of ridges; lenticular cloud can indicate wave activity. Do not treat a radar/satellite image as a complete go/no-go decision.

For planning, combine surface/upper-air charts, synoptic fronts, METAR/TAF, low-level area forecasts (including GAFOR where provided), low-level significant-weather charts, SIGMET/warnings, radar/satellite and winds aloft. GAFOR expresses regional VFR difficulty using forecast visibility/ceiling; decode the issuing service's zones, time blocks and legend. Radar depicts precipitation echoes, not all cloud or turbulence; satellite gives cloud coverage/top information, not runway visibility. Compare issue/validity times and route altitude; check trend, alternates and escape options. Forecast products describe a bounded area/time, not guaranteed conditions along the whole route.
