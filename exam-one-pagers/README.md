# EASA PPL(A) exam one-pagers

These are compact, topic-by-topic revision summaries for the nine EASA/Part-FCL PPL(A) theoretical-knowledge subjects, plus practical reference pages. The subject sheets follow the topics in the available Belgian course notes and question banks, explaining the main concepts, relationships, calculations, and distinctions rather than only giving general flight-safety reminders. They support study but do not replace the full course, current regulations, an operational checklist, or instructor guidance.

**Print:** [Download all summaries as one PDF](PPL-A-study-summaries.pdf). Each topic starts on a new page; longer summaries span multiple pages.

## How to use these notes

1. Learn the principle, then practise applying it to unfamiliar questions; do not memorize answers without understanding assumptions and units.
2. Check the current EASA rules and your competent authority's exam guidance for regulations and examination administration. National procedures, airspace, phraseology, and published minima can differ.
3. For an actual flight, use current NOTAMs/AIP, charts and weather products, the aircraft's current POH/AFM, and your instructor's approved procedures. Aircraft-specific limitations and approved data take precedence over generic examples here.

## Course alignment and source hierarchy

Each subject sheet links to its corresponding detailed lesson notes and question bank in this repository. Use those longer materials for diagrams, worked examples, local classroom conventions, and full question practice.

Regulatory statements are linked to EASA's [Easy Access Rules for Aircrew](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/easy-access-rules/easy-access-rules-aircrew) and [Standardised European Rules of the Air (SERA)](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/easy-access-rules/easy-access-rules-standardised-european-rules-air). These are consolidated references; check the underlying current regulation and competent-authority material for operational use. The UK CAA's [CAP 1298](https://www.caa.co.uk/data-and-publications/publications/documents/content/cap1298/) is an older syllabus cross-check, not the controlling rule set for an EASA flight. Aviation fundamentals are cross-checked against official FAA handbooks, the UK Met Office, or NIST where suitable. Links are supplied on each sheet.

These notes do not reproduce protected question-bank content. Regulations and publications can change; sources were consulted in November 2026.
