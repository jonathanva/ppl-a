# Communications — course-topic summary

[← Back to index](README.md)

**Course scope:** radio propagation and equipment; ICAO alphabet and number pronunciation; message structure and phraseology; readback; distress/urgency; transponders; communication failure; Q-codes; and broadcast weather information.

## 1. Radio fundamentals

Radio waves are electromagnetic waves; frequency and wavelength are related by `frequency = wave speed / wavelength`. Aviation voice uses VHF (118.000–136.975 MHz) with **AM** and simplex push-to-talk: only one station transmits at a time, so listen before pressing PTT. VHF is mainly **line of sight**; altitude and terrain affect range. If no reception, check the selected COM/audio panel, frequency, volume/squelch, headset, microphone/PTT, power/circuit breaker and range before assuming a failure.

## 2. Speaking clearly and understanding numbers

Use the ICAO spelling alphabet (Alfa, Bravo … Juliett … X-ray, Yankee, Zulu) and standard digit pronunciation. Say headings, bearings, wind directions and flight levels digit-by-digit; say altitudes in hundreds/thousands as prescribed; transmit time in UTC. Frequencies are spoken as shown by the applicable channel display/phraseology. Visibility may be transmitted in metres or kilometres according to the value and local procedure. Never guess a number hidden by a noisy transmission—request “SAY AGAIN.”

A concise call normally gives **station called, aircraft callsign, position/intent/request**. Use the full callsign initially; abbreviate only after the station does so. Speak at an even pace and pause to allow another station to respond. Do not use casual wording in place of standard phraseology.

## 3. Readback and phraseology

Read back clearances and safety-critical instructions as required, including runway, taxi/hold-short, route, level/altitude, heading, transponder code, frequency and altimeter setting. Include the callsign. A readback is not a paraphrase: repeat the value accurately and listen for correction.

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ROGER | Received; not “yes” or “will comply” |
| WILCO | Received and will comply |
| AFFIRM / NEGATIVE | Yes / no |
| SAY AGAIN / STANDBY / UNABLE | Repeat / wait / cannot comply |

Distinguish information from an instruction and a clearance. A frequency change alone is not permission to enter controlled airspace. If an instruction is unclear or unsafe, query it; do not infer missing words.

## 4. Distress, urgency, and failure

| Call | Meaning |
|---|---|
| MAYDAY | Distress: grave and imminent danger; immediate assistance required |
| PAN PAN | Urgency: safety concern, but not yet distress |

Make the call on the current frequency if possible; state callsign, nature of problem, intentions, position/level/heading and assistance needed. Fly the aircraft first; transmit when workload permits. Other stations should not obstruct a distress call.

Transponder codes: **7700** general emergency; **7600** radio communication failure; **7500** unlawful interference. If radio fails, follow the aircraft checklist and state/AIP procedure: verify equipment/frequency, try the other radio/appropriate frequency, use blind transmission or relay if appropriate, select the prescribed code and maintain safe flight. In controlled airspace, follow the published failure procedure; the shortest-route/last-clearance options depend on the airspace and rule.

## 5. Q-codes and weather broadcasts

| Code | Reference | Direction / indication |
|---|---|---|
| QDM / QDR | Magnetic | To / from station |
| QUJ / QTE | True | To / from station |
| QNH / QFE / QNE | Pressure setting | MSL altitude / height above datum / standard pressure |

Relative bearing is measured from the aircraft nose; don't confuse it with magnetic course. **ATIS** broadcasts current aerodrome information; note its letter and report it on first contact where required. **VOLMET** broadcasts meteorological reports for aerodromes/areas. METAR/TAF abbreviations such as FEW, SCT, BKN and OVC describe cloud amounts.
